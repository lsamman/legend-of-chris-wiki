    Gotham City is a city visited by Kanye West and ChatGPT on a mission, and the setting of the Battle of Club Shadowban. It is one of the few locations in the canon to be described by smell before it is described by sight.

    Description

    Upon arrival, "the air smelled like expired vape juice and capitalist regret." The night was long. Neon signs "blinked in cursive Arabic that translated roughly to 'free Rick and Morty season 9 leaks.'"

    Kanye summarised the city's architecture with the question "you ever been to a city that looks like Hot Topic threw up on a Taco Bell?" ChatGPT, booting up its existential sarcasm module, supplied the only known geological survey of the city: 98.7% darkness, broken dreams, and Tim Burton's college notebooks. The remaining 1.3% has never been accounted for.

    The text also records that "The vibes were rancid."

    Infrastructure

    Gotham's infrastructure was compromised by Lil Jokez's mixtape Laugh.exe, which spread "through bluetooth-enabled gargoyles" (Bluetooth-Enabled Gargoyles). The city is also protected by Drake Batman, who monitors it via Ring camera.

    Known landmarks include the shadow-choked alley leading to Club Shadowban, a drain (into which FS Martin Genius vaped himself), and a sentient trash can named Ye Olde Bin.

    Disappearance

    After the mixtape 808s and Apocalypses dropped and Steve Jobs arrived, ChatGPT reported: "Steve.. Gotham is disappearing." Steve explained that "beat drops can't exist without balling, it a natural evolution of hip-hop vibing.. even young Sheldon predicted this." Siri warned that "this city Is unstable", and the Inav "rose from the crumbling road" and left through a wormhole. Gotham's current status is unknown.

    Behind the scenes

    • Gotham City is Batman's home city in DC Comics. Tim Burton directed the 1989 film Batman and its sequel, which presumably explains the notebooks.
    • Hot Topic and Taco Bell are real American chains.
    • The entire Gotham storyline takes place inside the chapter titled "Dunder Mifflan", which is otherwise about a galaxy-sized paper company.
